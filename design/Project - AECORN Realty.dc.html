AECORN Realty

Using AI with identity design.

Summary

AECORN was a full-scale identity project for a tech-forward real estate brokerage. But instead of breaking down the branding elements, let's talk instead about how I used AI throughout this project to enhance creativity, output and process without sacrificing quality.

HOW AI IS USED
Web development Illustration & imagery Motion & video Social post process

All mockups represent real, existing assets.
Starting with an identity

The main identity is made up of your typical elements—logo, colour palette, fonts and visual direction. These were all developed by hand.

Where AI shines is not in the identity itself, but in how the assets are created, evolved and manipulated. AI does not make my decisions for me — it acts as an executional arm to my creative plan. AI empowers me to no longer be limited by a lack of skills in motion, illustration or web development.

Bringing in AI

The first place I implemented AI was on the business cards. We wanted to create personalized cards that would make an impact. So I came up with the idea of using each agent's portrait to make the cards feel welcoming and memorable.

The problem? We couldn't manage the process and cost of consistently doing professional photoshoots. So I researched the best AI tools for generating photo-realistic portraits based on real-life images. The results were fabulous, adaptable and cost effective.

AI designing an entire site

For the new AECORN site, we looked at many development options. We ended up going with a two-phase methodology. I would design the entire site with Claude, and hand over the entire package to the developer to produce a pixel perfect version from my files.

This meant I had to build a brand system inside Claude, while also iterating on design, refining layout, working out typography, defining hierarchy, and everything else you need to do when designing a site.

I went through the entire process inside AI. I created multiple spaces for experimentation, testing and brainstorming. I then pushed approved versions to a final environment. This allowed me to define and test interactions, motion and animation in real-time.

And because I was able to handover finalized designs down to the pixel, the developer was able to reproduce my work almost flawlessly.
